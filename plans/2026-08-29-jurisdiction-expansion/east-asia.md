# East Asia Web Accessibility Legal Landscape — China (Mainland), South Korea, Hong Kong, Macau, Mongolia

Research date: 2026-08-29. Japan and Taiwan excluded (covered elsewhere).

---

## 1. China (Mainland, PRC)

**Statute**: 中华人民共和国无障碍环境建设法 (Law of the PRC on Accessibility Environment Construction / Barrier-Free Environment Construction Law). Passed by the Standing Committee of the 14th National People's Congress on 2023-06-28; effective 2023-09-01 (Art. 72). This sits alongside the older 2012 State Council regulation 无障碍环境建设条例 (State Council Order No. 622, 2012), which was administrative-only and pre-dated internet-specific coverage.
Confidence: verified (read via official gov.cn text summary).
Source: gov.cn official text — https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202306/content_6888910.htm

**Scope, public vs private**:
- Chapter 3, 无障碍信息交流 (Accessible Information Exchange), governs digital/information accessibility specifically.
- Art. 32: government-funded (财政资金建立的) websites, service platforms, and mobile apps must ("应当") progressively comply with accessibility design standards and national information-accessibility standards. Mandatory for publicly funded digital services.
- Art. 32 also encourages (not mandates) private-sector apps in news, social media, e-commerce, healthcare, finance, education, and transport to progressively meet accessibility standards. Soft obligation for private sector.
- Art. 33: device/hardware manufacturers encouraged to progressively add accessibility features.
Confidence: verified for Art. 32/33 existence and general thrust (via WebFetch of official summary); exact article wording is a paraphrase/translation, not a direct primary-source string comparison. Treat exact wording as secondary-level precision pending direct gazette-PDF read.

**Technical standard**: GB/T 37668-2019, 信息技术 互联网内容无障碍可访问性技术要求与测试方法 (Information technology, Internet content accessibility technical requirements and conformance testing). Published 2019-08-30, effective 2020-03-01. Developed jointly by China Disabled Persons' Federation and Zhejiang University, referencing WCAG 2.0 and 2.1. Applies to web pages (incl. mobile web) and mobile app products.
Binding status: GB/T = recommended (推荐性) national standard, not mandatory (强制性). It becomes binding in practice only where another instrument (like Art. 32's "应当") requires compliance, or via procurement/certification requirements.
Confidence: verified (official SAMR standard registry page).
Sources: SAMR standard registry — https://openstd.samr.gov.cn/bzgk/std/newGbInfo?hcno=35ECC696805C1A67C93B74FB6D0D8EFB ; W3C blog on the 2019 update — https://www.w3.org/zh-hans/blog/2020/updated-chinese-accessibility-standard/

**Enforcement**:
- Art. 65: relevant authorities (housing/urban-rural construction, civil affairs, transport depts.) order correction within a deadline; if uncorrected, fines of RMB 10,000-30,000 for entities, RMB 100-500 for individuals.
- Art. 66: telecom-sector violations result in public criticism/circulated notices.
- Art. 67: telecom operators face RMB 10,000-100,000 fines for non-compliance.
Confidence: secondary-only. These figures come from a WebFetch-summarized secondary rendering of the gazette text, not a direct line-by-line read of the promulgated PDF. Spot-check against the primary gazette PDF before citing as exact figures in a compliance product.
Real enforcement/litigation example: none found in this research pass. The law is under two years old (effective Sept 2023); no accessibility-specific court judgment or public enforcement action under this law was located. State this as "no known cases yet," not "no enforcement mechanism."

Sources:
- 中华人民共和国无障碍环境建设法, gov.cn — https://www.gov.cn/yaowen/liebiao/202306/content_6888910.htm
- 人民网 official text mirror — http://politics.people.com.cn/n1/2023/0629/c1001-40023507.html
- GB/T 37668-2019, SAMR openstd registry — https://openstd.samr.gov.cn/bzgk/std/newGbInfo?hcno=35ECC696805C1A67C93B74FB6D0D8EFB
- GB/T 37668-2019, SAMR std search entry — https://std.samr.gov.cn/gb/search/gbDetailed?id=91890A0DA54C80C6E05397BE0A0A065D

**Verdict**: strong-specific-law (2023 statute with a dedicated information-accessibility chapter plus a national technical standard referencing WCAG 2.0/2.1), but mandatory force is currently limited to publicly funded digital services; private sector is "encouraged," not compelled; fines are modest and no case law yet exists.

## 2. South Korea

Two statutes work together; do not cite only one.

**Statute A (general anti-discrimination, web-accessibility-relevant)**: 장애인차별금지 및 권리구제 등에 관한 법률 (Act on the Prohibition of Discrimination Against Persons with Disabilities and Remedies, commonly "장애인차별금지법"). Enacted 2007-04-10, effective 2008-04-11; amended repeatedly since. Exact latest amendment date not independently confirmed this pass, flagged below.
- Art. 20 (정보접근에서의 차별금지, non-discrimination in information access): prohibits individuals, corporations, and public institutions from discriminating against persons with disabilities in the use of and access to electronic and non-electronic information; also protects those assisting disabled persons' communication (sign interpreters, Braille transcribers, readers, scribes) from obstruction or unfair treatment.
- Art. 21 (정보통신·의사소통 등에서의 정당한 편의제공의무, duty to provide reasonable accommodation in information/telecom and communication): requires providers offering information/services via telecom networks to furnish the "reasonable accommodation" needed for disabled persons to access and use them equally to non-disabled persons.
- Penalty: if the National Human Rights Commission of Korea investigates and finds discriminatory conduct, and a Ministry of Justice corrective order is issued but not complied with, an administrative fine (과태료) of up to KRW 30,000,000 may be imposed.
Confidence: secondary-only. Sourced from search-engine summaries of law.go.kr / LBOX / CaseNote citing Articles 20-21 and the KRW 30M figure consistently across independent secondary legal-database sites, but WebFetch could not render law.go.kr's JS-based page directly, so this is not a direct primary-source read this pass.
Sources: 법제처 국가법령정보센터 (law.go.kr) — https://www.law.go.kr/%EB%B2%95%EB%A0%B9/%EC%9E%A5%EC%95%A0%EC%9D%B8%EC%B0%A8%EB%B3%84%EA%B8%88%EC%A7%80%EB%B0%8F%EA%B6%8C%EB%A6%AC%EA%B5%AC%EC%A0%9C%EB%93%B1%EC%97%90%EA%B4%80%ED%95%9C%EB%B2%95%EB%A5%A0 ; LBOX Art. 21 citation — https://lbox.kr/v2/statute/%EC%9E%A5%EC%95%A0%EC%9D%B8%EC%B0%A8%EB%B3%84%EA%B8%88%EC%A7%80%EB%B0%8F%EA%B6%8C%EB%A6%AC%EA%B5%AC%EC%A0%9C%EB%93%B1%EC%97%90%EA%B4%80%ED%95%9C%EB%B2%95%EB%A5%A0 ; Korean Wikisource full text — https://ko.wikisource.org/wiki/%EC%9E%A5%EC%95%A0%EC%9D%B8%EC%B0%A8%EB%B3%84%EA%B8%88%EC%A7%80_%EB%B0%8F_%EA%B6%8C%EB%A6%AC%EA%B5%AC%EC%A0%9C_%EB%93%B1%EC%97%90_%EA%B4%80%ED%95%9C_%EB%B2%95%EB%A5%A0_(%EC%A0%9C10789%ED%98%B8)

**Statute B (technical mandate for public sector plus KWCAG basis)**: 지능정보화기본법 (National Intelligent Informatization Act / Framework Act on Intelligent Informatization). Art. 46 (장애인·고령자 등의 지능정보서비스 접근 및 이용 보장, ensuring accessibility of intelligent-information services for the disabled/elderly):
- National/public agencies (국가기관등) providing information or services via telecom networks must ("보장하여야 한다") ensure disabled/elderly users can access websites and mobile apps. This is the binding public-sector mandate.
- Intelligent-information-service providers (broader, including private) are only obligated to "endeavor" (노력하여야 한다), a best-efforts, not a hard, duty.
- Manufacturers of related ICT/intelligent-information products likewise only "endeavor."
Confidence: verified for existence and general structure (law.go.kr search-result snippet directly quotes the operative "보장하여야 한다" vs "노력하여야 한다" distinction), but exact current article numbering/text was not read in a rendered primary page this pass. Treat as high-confidence secondary.
Source: law.go.kr 지능정보화기본법 제46조 — https://www.law.go.kr/%EB%B2%95%EB%A0%B9/%EC%A7%80%EB%8A%A5%EC%A0%95%EB%B3%B4%ED%99%94%EA%B8%B0%EB%B3%B8%EB%B2%95/(20220721,18298,20210720)/%EC%A0%9C46%EC%A1%B0 ; CaseNote mirror — https://casenote.kr/%EB%B2%95%EB%A0%B9/%EC%A7%80%EB%8A%A5%EC%A0%95%EB%B3%B4%ED%99%94_%EA%B8%B0%EB%B3%B8%EB%B2%95/%EC%A0%9C46%EC%A1%B0

**Technical standard**: KWCAG (한국형 웹 콘텐츠 접근성 지침) 2.2, updated 2022. Four principles, 14 guidelines, 33 success criteria (mirrors WCAG 2.x structure but is a distinct Korean standard, not a straight WCAG adoption). Explicitly positioned by its own documentation as the technical standard used to satisfy web-accessibility obligations under both 지능정보화기본법 and 장애인차별금지법.
Confidence: verified (official standard page and independent developer reference site cross-agree on structure).
Sources: 국립전파연구원(RRA) KWCAG page — https://www.rra.go.kr/ko/reference/kcsList_view.do?nb_seq=5247&nb_type=6 ; KWCAG 2.2 reference implementation site — https://a11ykr.github.io/kwcag22/

**Enforcement**: administrative fine up to KRW 30,000,000 under 장애인차별금지법 (see above) following NHRCK investigation plus Ministry of Justice corrective order route. No specific web-accessibility litigation case was independently verified in this pass. South Korea is known in secondary accessibility-industry literature for periodic screen-reader-user lawsuits against government/bank sites, but this pass did not locate and verify a specific named case against a primary court record; do not cite an unverified case name.
Confidence: uncertain on case examples; stating "none independently verified" rather than fabricating a case name.

**Verdict**: strong-specific-law, dual-statute regime (general anti-discrimination plus a dedicated ICT-framework act), binding public-sector web/app accessibility mandate, named national technical standard (KWCAG 2.2), and a quantified penalty ceiling, though private-sector duty is best-efforts only and this pass could not verify a named enforcement case.

## 3. Hong Kong SAR

**Statute**: Disability Discrimination Ordinance (Cap. 487), "DDO", enacted 1995, in force since 1996. General anti-discrimination ordinance, not a web/digital-specific statute.
- Sections 6, 25, 26, 27: unlawful to discriminate against a person with a disability in provision of goods, services, or facilities (Section 26 is the core "goods, services or facilities" provision); Section 25 covers access to premises; Section 27 extends to specific facility types (e.g., toilets). No section names websites, apps, or ICT explicitly; digital services fall under the general "services" language by interpretation, not explicit text.
Confidence: secondary-only. WebFetch could not render elegislation.gov.hk or HKLII (both JS-loaded); section numbers and characterization above are cross-corroborated from EOC's own published guidance and law-firm summaries, not a direct read of the ordinance's PDF/HTML.
Sources: EOC, Disability Discrimination law page — https://www.eoc.org.hk/en/discrimination-laws/disability-discrimination ; EOC Universal Design Practical Guide, citing ss.6/25/26 — https://www.eoc.org.hk/Upload/files/Policy%20Frameworks%20and%20Guidelines/UD%20Guide%20Oct%202023/EOC_UD_Guide_text_eng.pdf ; Refworld, DDO 1995 consolidated text — https://www.refworld.org/legal/decreees/natlegbod/1995/en/13657

**Technical standard, public sector only**: "Guidelines on Dissemination of Information through Government Websites", a Digital Policy Office (formerly OGCIO) administrative policy (per W3C WAI's policy tracker, originally dating to 1999), mandating WCAG 2.0 Level AA for government websites. This is binding on government bureaux/departments as internal administrative policy, not statute; it does not derive from the DDO and has no independent penalty provision of its own. Roughly 650 government websites are reported as WCAG 2.0 AA-conformant. There is no binding requirement for the private sector; a voluntary "Digital Accessibility Campaign" / Web Accessibility Recognition Scheme encourages private-sector adoption without legal force.
Confidence: verified for existence and WCAG 2.0 AA / government-only / non-statutory-policy characterization (W3C WAI's official policy tracker page, which itself cites the Digital Policy Office).
Sources: W3C WAI, Hong Kong SAR policy page — https://www.w3.org/WAI/policies/hong-kong-hksar/ ; Digital Policy Office, accessibility resources — https://www.digitalpolicy.gov.hk/en/our_work/digital_government/digital_inclusion/accessibility/promulgating_resources/handbook/related_resources/

**Enforcement**: DDO complaints are handled by the Equal Opportunities Commission (EOC), which can conciliate, or (if conciliation fails) the complainant/EOC can bring proceedings in the District Court, which may award damages (courts apply a Vento-scale-derived banding: roughly HK$9,500-95,000 lowest band up to HK$285,000-475,000 top band for injury to feelings, per EOC's own case summaries, plus economic loss/costs/interest). This pass found substantiated employment- and service-refusal disability-discrimination cases (e.g., an EOC-assisted District Court claim over dismissal linked to disability) but no website/digital-accessibility-specific DDO case; state this as "no web-accessibility case found," not "DDO has never been enforced."
Confidence: verified for the damages-banding mechanism and general DDO litigation route (EOC's own significant-cases page); uncertain/no case found specifically for websites.
Sources: EOC, Significant Court Cases: Disability Discrimination — https://www.eoc.org.hk/en/legal-services/significant-court-cases/hong-kong/disability-discrimination ; EOC, Legal Assistance — https://www.eoc.org.hk/en/legal-services/legal-assistance

**Verdict**: framework-no-web-specifics. General disability-discrimination ordinance with no explicit web/ICT text; a real but non-statutory, government-only WCAG 2.0 AA mandate; private sector has no binding obligation; no web-specific enforcement case identified.

## 4. Macau SAR

**Statute**: no disability-discrimination or web-accessibility statute was found. Macau's disability framework is scattered across social-welfare instruments (e.g., Law 9/2011, disability allowance/free healthcare regime) rather than an anti-discrimination or accessibility-construction law comparable to mainland China's 2023 statute or Hong Kong's DDO.
Confidence: verified as an absence claim within this pass's search coverage. Multiple targeted searches (Chinese and English) for a Macau accessibility/anti-discrimination statute, and for a specific web-accessibility law, returned only non-binding guidance documents and general disability-welfare instruments. This is a negative finding from search coverage, not a certified exhaustive legal-database check; recommend a follow-up direct check of Macau's official gazette portal (bo.io.gov.mo) before treating as final.
Sources checked: 澳門法律網 (macaolaw.gov.mo) — https://www.macaolaw.gov.mo/ ; 法務局 法例 index — https://www.bo.dsaj.gov.mo/cn/legis

**CRPD status**: China ratified the CRPD in 2008 with no reservations, and consistent with China's general treaty-extension practice for its two SARs, the Convention applies to Macao SAR. Macao's Social Welfare Bureau and Legal Affairs Bureau participate directly in periodic CRPD Committee reviews as part of China's combined state reports (documented for the 2022 review cycle).
Confidence: verified for participation in CRPD reporting (OHCHR meeting record naming Macao's delegation) but not independently verified via the primary UN treaty-status table that Macao is explicitly named in China's ratification/declaration instrument (this pass's UN Treaty Collection searches did not surface the exact declaration text). Treat CRPD-applies-to-Macao as high-confidence-secondary, not primary-verified.
Source: OHCHR, 2022 review of China/HKSAR/MSAR — https://www.ohchr.org/en/news/2022/07/experts-human-rights-committee-commend-macao-special-administrative-region-china (note: this specific link covers the Human Rights Committee/ICCPR review, cited here to confirm Macao's standard treaty-reporting participation pattern; the CRPD-specific 2022 review record is a companion OHCHR release not independently re-verified this pass, flag for follow-up)

**Technical guidance, non-binding**: Macau published a 無障礙通用設計建築指引 (Accessibility Universal Design Building Guidelines), most recently updated 2021, covering the built environment, not websites. Separately, a 2014 政府部門網站規範指引 (Government Department Website Standards Guideline) reportedly includes accessibility elements (unified page structure, alt text for images/audio/animation, font-size adjustment) referencing W3C WCAG 2.0 as a baseline for some government sites (e.g., the Macau Public Library site). None of this carries legal force: secondary commentary (a local civil-society group, 傳新澳門協會) explicitly states these guidelines bind only new-construction/government-funded projects administratively and have no binding force on private enterprises or public-facing private venues, and one search result stated Macau has no guideline at all for mobile-app accessibility.
Confidence: secondary-only. Sourced from civil-society commentary and government portal search snippets, not a primary read of the 2014 guideline document itself (a direct copy of that guideline was not locatable in this pass).
Sources: 傳新澳門協會 commentary on the legal gap — https://www.synergymacao.org/2021/12/06/ronlam20211206a1/ ; 澳門特區無障礙通用設計建築指引 2021 (buildings, not web) — https://www.ias.gov.mo/wp-content/themes/ias/tw/download/2021-11-15_add3.pdf ; 澳門公共圖書館 accessibility features page — https://www.library.gov.mo/zh-hant/acc-website

**Enforcement**: none found. No statute means no statutory complaint mechanism or penalty specific to (web) accessibility. General discrimination/human-rights complaint channels may exist but were not the object of this search.

**Verdict**: honest-null. No specific web-accessibility law; CRPD applies via China's ratification (high-confidence, not primary-doc-verified); only non-binding administrative guidelines exist, and per local civil-society sources, an explicit legal gap is a recognized public criticism in Macau itself.

## 5. Mongolia

**Statute**: Law of Mongolia on the Rights of Persons with Disabilities, enacted by the State Great Khural (parliament) on 2016-02-05. This is a CRPD-aligned rights-based law (explicitly designed to move away from the earlier welfare/medical-model approach), covering accommodations, public facilities, roads, public transport, and, per secondary summaries, communication technology, with the government's information/communication authority tasked to ensure enforcement of relevant clauses in partnership with other organizations and to secure budget funding.
Confidence: secondary-only. The PDF of the law's official English translation was fetched but returned as unparseable binary content in this pass (raw PDF stream, not extracted text), so the ICT/communication-technology clause could not be confirmed by direct article-number citation. The "communication technology" content above comes from a secondary summary (disability-policy literature review), not a verified quote of a specific article number.
Sources: DREDF, English translation PDF of the 2016 law — https://dredf.org/wp-content/uploads/2018/10/The-Law-of-Mongolia-on-Human-Rights-of-Persons-with-Disabilities-02-05-16.pdf ; UN DESA mirror of the same translation — https://www.un.org/development/desa/disabilities/wp-content/uploads/sites/15/2019/11/Mongolia_Law-on-human-Rights-of-Persons-with-Disabilities.pdf

**Technical standard**: none found. No Mongolia-specific web-content accessibility standard (no "MWCAG" equivalent) was located. G3ict's own country profile states accessibility in ICT is "quite a new notion in Mongolia with very few professionals in the field," which corroborates the absence of a mature technical-standard regime.
Confidence: verified as an absence claim within this pass's search coverage (G3ict is a primary-adjacent authoritative tracker for CRPD/ICT accessibility policy globally).
Source: G3ict, Mongolia country profile — https://g3ict.org/country-profile/mongolia

**CRPD status**: Mongolia signed and ratified/acceded to the CRPD around 2008-2009 (secondary sources place signature in 2008 and accession in 2009; exact instrument date not independently pinned down against the UN Treaty Collection this pass). Mongolia underwent CRPD Committee review of its initial state report in 2015 and a further review cycle in 2023, where the Committee specifically asked about accessibility measures.
Confidence: verified for the fact of CRPD Committee reviews in 2015 and 2023 (direct OHCHR meeting-summary sources); secondary-only for exact ratification/accession year.
Sources: OHCHR, 2015 initial report review — https://www.ohchr.org/en/press-releases/2015/04/committee-rights-persons-disabilities-considers-initial-report-mongolia ; OHCHR, 2023 review, accessibility questions — https://www.ohchr.org/en/meeting-summaries/2023/08/experts-committee-rights-persons-disabilities-commend-mongolia

**Enforcement**: no accessibility-specific penalty/complaint mechanism located; Mongolia does report progress on physical/built-environment accessibility (a building/structure accessibility checklist in use since 2022, per the 2023 OHCHR review summary) but this is the built environment, not digital/web. No web-accessibility litigation or enforcement example was found.

**Verdict**: honest-null. A genuine disability-rights law exists (2016) and CRPD is ratified, but no web-specific accessibility law or technical standard exists, and Mongolia's own international reporting and G3ict's tracker both confirm ICT accessibility is at an early/nascent stage.
