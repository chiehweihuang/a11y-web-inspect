# Real-site survey snapshot: 3,700 sites

Snapshot date: 2026-08-13. Canonical artifacts live in the local measurement
workspace; this committed summary records the reproducible cohort definition and the
claims it can support without publishing captured third-party pages.

## Cohort

| Measure | Count |
|---|---:|
| Registered survey targets processed | 5,716 |
| Unique sites with a successful rendered snapshot and audit | **3,700** |
| Matched snapshot files | 3,700 |
| Matched audit JSON files | 3,700 |
| Current active analysis cohort | **3,625** |
| Successfully audited artifacts excluded from the active cohort | 75 |
| Current registry status: walled | 608 |
| Current registry status: dead/failed | 1,483 |

All 3,700 successful audits were normalized to
`beacon-static-audit@18+ruleset.bde99ddc0571`. Bot-wall and failed captures do not
produce survey audit artifacts and do not enter detector, score, coverage, or finding
statistics. A site successfully audited earlier can later be marked non-active; this is
why the active cohort is smaller than the artifact cohort.

The survey uses a deterministic, rank-stratified sample from the Tranco list. Capture
uses Chromium at 1280x900, locale `en-US`, `domcontentloaded`, a network-idle grace
period, and a two-second settle before saving rendered HTML. URLs and captured pages are
not committed here because they can contain third-party content and change over time.

## What the cohort is for

1. **Detector precision sampling.** Draw flags from distinct sites, stratified by
   detector, language, finding volume, and score band. Human adjudication, not the raw
   3,700-site count, establishes precision.
2. **False-positive discovery.** High-volume keys and outlier sites identify root-cause
   classes to investigate. Counts are candidate queues, not confirmed defect prevalence.
3. **Release movement.** Before a detector or scoring minor release, rerun the same
   snapshots and explain every changed finding key, state, coverage value, and score.
4. **Regression selection.** Keep a small frozen corpus in CI and select it from this
   mother cohort across languages, detector keys, score bands, and known FP classes.
5. **Capture drift.** Recapture a rotating subset on another day or machine to separate
   page/capture changes from deterministic engine changes.
6. **Fairness probes.** Use language and technology strata to find disparities that need
   matched follow-up tests. Raw average scores by language are confounded by site mix and
   are not fairness conclusions.

## What it does not prove

- It is not 3,700 manually adjudicated accessibility audits.
- It does not establish an overall accuracy, precision, recall, or compliance rate.
- The 75,625 emitted finding instances include review evidence, repeated template
  instances, and false positives; they are not 75,625 confirmed barriers.
- Score distributions describe Beacon's static evidence on this cohort, not the
  accessibility quality of the web.
- Bot-wall and failed-capture rates measure pipeline reach, not detector quality.

## Evidence layers

| Evidence | Role |
|---|---|
| 3,700 successful real-site audits | mother cohort for discovery, stratification, and release movement |
| 3,625 active audits | current aggregate-analysis cohort after artifact/status exclusions |
| 40 frozen real pages in `test/wild-corpus/` | deterministic CI regression |
| Six detector samples, 15 instances each | current published human-adjudicated precision evidence |
| 20-site ground truth | current precision/recall inventory |
| 87-site paired benchmark, 71 usable pairs | external relationship study |

The next validation gain is not collecting more undifferentiated sites. It is increasing
the human-adjudicated sample for high-volume or low-precision detectors, then using the
remaining survey pool as an untouched holdout for the next measurement round.
